The Core Idea
Students in Schuylkill County should graduate knowing this place — its ecosystems, its history, its industries, its future. And they should leave equipped to contribute to it.

IU29 is the regional education agency for all 12 public school districts in Schuylkill County — the organization that sits between the state and local schools and makes county-wide collaboration possible. We don't own the classrooms. We don't set the curriculum. But we broker the connections, coordinate the logistics, and make sure a great idea in Mahanoy City can also reach a student in Williams Valley.

What does that infrastructure look like? A Tamaqua 6th grader collecting water samples from the Little Schuylkill alongside a Conservation District scientist. A Williams Valley teacher and a Blue Mountain teacher planning the same field experience with a local environmental researcher — because IU29 made the introduction.

We don't own the work. We make the work work together.

Every kind of partner has a different stake in what we're building. Here's what it means for yours.

Conservation & Environment

For Conservation & Environmental Partners

The next stewards of the Schuylkill River, Hawk Mountain, and our conservation lands are sitting in classrooms right now. They don't know it yet — because no one's introduced them to the place they live in. Through structured field partnerships — ones we're actively building — students can run real water quality tests on the Schuylkill, engage with raptor migration data, and participate in watershed work with the Conservation District. We can build those moments together — before students leave. Field experiences with real scientists build the emotional connection that creates lifelong advocates — and your next generation of staff.

Business & Workforce

For Business & Workforce Partners

Schuylkill County's employers tell us the same thing: they can't find enough qualified people locally. The pipeline starts leaking before graduation — it starts when students can't picture themselves in a career that exists down the road. The "brain drain" begins with disconnection. Students who tour a plant floor, shadow an engineer, or build something real for a local company are measurably more likely to see a future here. Your company doesn't need to build a program. You need to show up once. STEM literacy built around local industries is workforce development that starts in 6th grade.

Teachers & Districts

For Teachers & Districts

Pennsylvania's STEELS standards require three-dimensional, phenomenon-based science instruction. That's not an add-on — it's a fundamental shift in how science gets taught. And it's harder to execute when you're already stretched. The ecosystem doesn't add to your prep burden — it adds to your options: field sites, industry partners, working scientists, all coordinated and curriculum-aligned. We handle the logistics. You bring the students. One good ecosystem connection can fuel two or three units of study that you didn't have to build alone.

Workforce Agencies & County

For Workforce Agencies & County Organizations

Connecting students to careers isn't just a school job. It takes CareerLink, county planning offices, adult education programs, and workforce development agencies working alongside schools — not parallel to them. IU29 is the bridge between education and economic development in Schuylkill County. If your agency is trying to build talent pipelines, address workforce gaps, or reach students before they leave — this ecosystem is the most direct route in. We coordinate the school relationships so you don't have to start from scratch with 12 different school districts.

What This Actually Looks Like

Partnership in practice.

Not a framework. Not a flow chart. Here's what happens when an organization decides to show up.

Manufacturing Partner
Two hours at a competition. One conversation that changed a career trajectory.

A regional manufacturer sends one staff member to judge the PA Keystone STEM Competition — two hours on a Saturday. She evaluates three student teams presenting engineering design solutions. She leaves with two business cards from teachers who want to bring students to the plant floor. By May, 22 students from two districts have done a half-day industry tour. None of them had ever been inside a manufacturing facility before.

What it cost: one day of one person's time.
Conservation Partner
A sanctuary has data. A Tamaqua 7th grader has a question. IU29 makes the introduction.

IU29 works with a 7th grade science teacher to build a three-week unit around the fall raptor migration count. Students learn to use eBird, practice field observation protocols, and connect with a local conservation researcher during peak migration week. The unit satisfies three STEELS performance expectations. The teacher didn't build it alone — she built it with us. This is the kind of partnership we're actively working to make real.

What it would cost: one planning conversation and one site visit.
Library Partner
The library had the community trust. IU29 had the programming. Neither could do it alone.

IU29 brings makerspace materials and a facilitator for four Saturday events. The library brings the families. 63 kids show up to the first "Build It" session — the most families the director has seen on a Saturday in years. Several parents ask how to connect their child to school STEM programs. The library gets programming. IU29 gets reach.

What it cost: an open door and a couple of hours.

How Partnership Works

Start simple.
Grow intentionally.

No giant commitment required to start. Every ecosystem partnership begins with a conversation and a small first step.

01
Tell Us Your Story
A 30-minute conversation — no pitch, no agenda, no commitment. Tell us about your organization, what you care about, and what you have capacity for. Most partners are surprised by how many connection points already exist. We've had this conversation with conservation scientists, factory floor managers, university deans, and librarians. The starting point is always the same: what do you wish students understood about what you do?
02
Start With One Thing
We identify one concrete first step based on what you actually told us — not a pre-packaged program option. A classroom visit. A competition judging slot. A shared field site. One genuine touchpoint is worth more than a year of "strategic alignment." We build the bridge; you walk across it first and see if it's worth crossing again.
03
Let It Compound
The partnerships that matter most start small and grow organically. A judging appearance becomes an advisory role. A field site becomes a co-designed curriculum unit. A career conversation becomes a job shadow program. We don't push the pace. We follow your capacity and your conviction — and keep making it easy to stay connected.

The Vision
We want to build a Generator — students who leave Schuylkill County to gain skills and perspective, then come back to the Skook to innovate from the inside out. What was once here, we bring back in new ways.
— Greg Macer, Supervisor of STEM & District Support, IU29

This isn't a strategic vision statement. It's what happens when you grow up here and want something better for the kids who come after you.

Skook Fact

"Schuylkill" is derived from Dutch, meaning hidden river — named by early European explorers who struggled to find the river's mouth through the dense marshlands. A hidden river. A hidden potential. We're here to uncover both.
